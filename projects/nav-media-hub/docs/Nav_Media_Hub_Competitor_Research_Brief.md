# Nav Media Hub — competitor research and one-page demo brief

**Research date:** 23 September 2026  
**Phase:** Research and design direction; no development started

## 1. What is verified about the client

The public [Nav Media Hub Instagram profile](https://www.instagram.com/navmediahub/) identifies the company as a marketing agency in Canada and describes more than four years creating cinematic visuals for cars, real estate, and social media. Its bio links to `navmediahub.com`. A [public 2025 post](https://www.instagram.com/navmediahub/p/DNw28ZGWML9/) specifically offers real estate photography, social media content creation, and drone/video walkthroughs to real estate agents and small businesses. A [public 2026 reel](https://www.instagram.com/navmediahub/reel/Dcgl96HsM6a/) describes premium video production paired with a broader digital marketing system. These are the company's own descriptions, not independently verified claims about results.

The domain returned an error during this research session. That observation alone does not establish why the existing site is unavailable or whether it fails for every visitor.

**Portfolio limitation:** Instagram showed only a small number of public posts without signing in. The reel's caption and preview were accessible, but the complete video portfolio, logo source file, and full visual identity could not be reliably audited. Obtain the original media and logo before final visual approval; do not lift compressed Instagram thumbnails as the production assets.

**Working audience hypothesis:** Canadian businesses seeking cinematic content, especially real estate agents and automotive brands. Confirm the actual service area and whether Nav Media Hub wants to prioritize one segment before finalizing copy.

## 3. Proposed one-page experience

1. **Header:** Proper logo file, anchors to Work / Services / Process / Contact, and a clear “Start a project” action. Keep mobile navigation small.
2. **Hero with inquiry form:** A strong headline such as “Make your next project impossible to overlook,” one sentence specifying cinematic video and content for brands in the approved service area, a selected video still or short muted showreel, and the working form. On mobile, show the headline and CTA first, followed closely by the form.
3. **Selected work:** Three to six *client-approved* videos, with meaningful project labels, industry, format and a one-line objective or deliverable. Lead with the strongest real estate and automotive examples; include small business/social content if approved. Click to play, with a useful poster image. Preserve vertical and horizontal aspect ratios rather than forcing all footage into identical crops.
4. **Services:** Compact categories based on the final approved offer, such as Real Estate Media, Automotive Films, Short-Form Social Content, and Brand Video. Only add drone, photography, paid campaigns or marketing automation if the client confirms they are currently available.
5. **How it works:** A concise three-step sequence: Tell us about the project → Plan and shoot → Edit and deliver. Validate actual workflow and turnaround with the client.
6. **Trust and second CTA:** Authentic client quote or approved brands/projects if available, then a repeated “Start a project” link back to the form. No invented testimonials, counts or growth figures.
7. **Footer:** Approved contact details, Instagram link, service area and privacy information.

### Visual direction

Use an editorial, cinematic layout: strong typography, generous spacing, large media frames and minimal decoration. A dark charcoal and warm neutral base is a **provisional** direction that works for both property and automotive footage; choose one restrained accent from the actual logo once supplied. Let real work provide the color and motion. Use deliberate hover/motion cues, but keep text readable over media and honor reduced-motion preferences. Show a poster while a video loads; avoid automatic audio and unnecessarily heavy full-page video backgrounds.

### Hero form, user journey and admin

**Visitor fields:** name, email, phone (optional unless the client wants calls as the primary follow-up), service interest, and a short project description. The form should identify required fields, validate email, show clear submitting/success/error states, and tell the visitor what happens next using a client-approved response expectation. Add a concise consent/privacy notice and basic spam protection.

**After submission:** store the inquiry, show an on-page confirmation, and make it visible in a protected admin panel. An optional notification email is useful, but the stored record should remain the primary record if email delivery fails.

**Admin minimum:** authenticated access; newest-first inquiry list; name, contact, service, message, submission time and status; view details; mark new/contacted/closed; and a clear empty state. The admin panel is a separate private interface, while the marketing site remains one public page. Do not expose submissions in the public page or browser-only storage.

## 4. Why this direction is stronger for the brief

- **Immediate path to inquiry:** the form is visible at the point of first interest, rather than living only on a contact page or in the footer.
- **Proof close to the promise:** selected, attributable videos follow the hero so a visitor can judge the quality quickly.
- **Clear choice of services:** real estate, automotive and other brand work can be browsed without a vague “we do everything” pitch.
- **A functional demo:** the client can submit a test inquiry and see it in the protected admin panel, which proves the requested workflow as well as the design.

These are design hypotheses drawn from the competitor audit. The actual conversion rate depends on traffic, media quality, offer and follow-up.
